\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Autonomous racing on edge devices requires highly optimized algorithms to overcome the latency constraints inherent to heavy deep learning models. This proposal presents a deterministic navigation architecture tailored for the Jetson AI Racer Challenge, divided into two distinct modules. For the Speed Track, we introduce the V3.1 High-Speed Vision architecture, which fuses robust Distance-Based Dual-Zone HSV Segmentation with Bird's Eye View (BEV) geometry and a Look-Ahead Horizon Automata to decouple continuous steering control from strategic speed planning. For the Smart City challenge, we employ an event-driven Finite State Machine (FSM) combining YOLOv8 instance segmentation and Hough Circle Transform to robustly extract and classify circular traffic signs at complex intersections. By separating tactical trajectory tracking from discrete safety and intersection behaviors, the proposed architecture is designed to improve control stability, optimize lap times, and maintain real-time performance on edge-computing platforms.}
\end{abstract}


\vspace{1em}
\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} Autonomous Racing, Bird's Eye View, PID Control, Look-Ahead Automata, Edge AI.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction \& Motivation}
Real-time navigation in autonomous miniature vehicles requires a delicate balance between perception accuracy and computational efficiency. The motivation for this research stems from the hardware constraints of edge computing platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson), where executing complex semantic segmentation networks often leads to frame rate drops and thermal throttling. Consequently, this study proposes an optimized deterministic methodology relying on traditional geometric computer vision and structured control theory to achieve high-speed, robust autonomous racing.

\subsection{Problem Statement \& Research Questions}
The primary challenge is maintaining stable lane tracking at maximum possible speeds while safely decelerating for curves, alongside navigating complex urban intersections. This requires a reliable perception-to-control pipeline. This proposal addresses the following research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How does a Distance-Based Dual-Zone HSV segmentation approach improve the robustness of look-ahead feature extraction against track illumination anomalies compared to uniform thresholds?
    \item How can a Look-Ahead Horizon Automata, when decoupled from tactical steering, optimize lap times without violating the vehicle's lateral acceleration limits?
    \item To what extent does asymmetric Exponential Moving Average (EMA) speed filtering prevent traction loss during rapid state transitions in high-speed racing?
\end{enumerate}

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\section{Related Work}
Current approaches to autonomous edge navigation generally fall into two categories:
\textbf{Deep Learning (End-to-End \& Segmentation):} Models like ResNet-18 or YOLOv8 offer robust generalization \cite{bojarski2016end}. However, even with TensorRT quantization, they consume significant VRAM and struggle with deterministic state transitions during sudden track variations.
\textbf{Traditional CV, PID Control \& Sensor-Based Navigation:} Lightweight computer vision remains a practical solution for small-scale autonomous robots because it can support real-time lane tracking with lower computational cost. Shamoushaki and Ghasemi applied Gaussian Blur, masking, and image moments for line detection in differential-drive mobile robots \cite{shamoushaki2024image}. Similarly, Farkh and Aljaloud proposed a vision-navigation line tracking robot where the error between the camera frame center and the detected line contour center is used as input to a PID controller for steering \cite{farkh2023vision}. 

\textit{Identified Gap:} Although previous works demonstrate effective lightweight vision processing and PID control, there is a lack of hybrid architectures that seamlessly integrate the spatial geometry of perspective-transformed vision with a decoupled strategic speed automata for high-speed curve anticipation, nor do they effectively address illumination vulnerabilities using dynamically adjusting threshold zones.

\section{Proposed Method}
Our system architecture relies on the duality of continuous control loops for lane keeping and discrete state automata for safety transitions. The methodology is divided into two primary domains: High-Speed Navigation for the Speed Track, and Semantic Automata for the Smart City.

\subsection{Speed Track: High-Speed Vision-Based Navigation (V3.1 Architecture)}
For the Speed Track challenge, relying solely on end-to-end Deep Learning may introduce latency issues, while basic PID controllers often lack the predictive capability required for competitive high-speed racing. To optimize lap time while maintaining reliable stability, we propose the \textbf{V3.1 Architecture}, a structured, multi-stage hybrid pipeline.

\subsubsection{Distance-Based Dual-Zone HSV Segmentation}
Traditional HSV-based color segmentation can be susceptible to illumination degradation and floor glare in the distal (far) regions of the camera frame. To address this, we implement a \textbf{Distance-Based Dual-Zone Segmentation} mechanism based on the camera's extrinsic pitch angle:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Proximal Zone (Bottom 45\%):} Utilizes a strict HSV threshold to extract lane markings reliably. The physical shadow of the vehicle stabilizes lighting in this region, reducing the need for aggressive filtering.
    \item \textbf{Distal Zone (Top 55\%):} Implements a dynamically relaxed HSV boundary to capture distant, faded lines under variable track lighting. Because the distal zone is highly susceptible to external glare, the relaxed threshold maintains feature continuity for the Horizon Scanner without introducing computational overhead from heavier color spaces like CIE-LAB.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Bird's Eye View Transform \& Trajectory Extraction}
The segmented binary mask undergoes a Perspective Transform to generate a Bird's Eye View (BEV), geometrically aligning the lane markings to be approximately parallel. A Histogram-based Sliding Window algorithm extracts the spatial coordinates of the lane.
Instead of merely calculating a static Cross-Track Error (CTE), the system derives the localized track curvature $\kappa$ using a second-order polynomial fit. The pixel-space coordinates are first converted into physical metric space ($x_m, y_m$) using camera-specific extrinsic scaling factors ($S_x, S_y$) to ensure physical validity:
\begin{equation}
    x_m = A y_m^2 + B y_m + C \implies \kappa = \frac{|2A|}{(1 + (2A y_m + B)^2)^{3/2}}
\end{equation}
This effective curvature $\kappa_{eff}$ represents the geometric track curvature in metric coordinates (1/m) and is buffered over a short rolling temporal window ($N=3$ frames) to suppress high-frequency noise while minimizing control delay.

\subsubsection{Continuous PID Steering Control}
The tactical steering angle $\delta(t)$ of the vehicle is regulated continuously by a Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller. The Cross-Track Error $e(t)$ is calculated as the lateral deviation from the desired lane center in the BEV space. To improve curve tracking, we introduce a curvature feed-forward term:
\begin{equation}
    \delta(t) = K_p \cdot e(t) + K_d \cdot \frac{de(t)}{dt} + K_{ff} \cdot \kappa_{eff}
\end{equation}
The integral term ($K_i$) is intentionally omitted to prevent integrator windup during high-speed oscillatory maneuvers.

\subsubsection{Look-Ahead Horizon Scanner \& Fail-Safe Automata}
To achieve competitive lap times, the vehicle must accelerate proactively on straightaways. To optimize computational efficiency on the Edge device while maximizing predictive capability, we introduce the \textbf{Distal Presence-Based Automata}. This ROI is empirically positioned far ahead of the vehicle. 

The geometric properties of perspective projection dictate that a straight track will converge vertically. Thus, rather than fitting computationally expensive polynomials or relying on arbitrary lateral shifts, the scanner isolates the largest lane contour and employs a Least-Squares Linear Regression (\texttt{cv2.fitLine}) to extract its tangent angle $\theta_{line}$ relative to the vertical axis. 

For the vehicle to safely accelerate, the track must be robustly visible ($\Sigma_{px} > 50$) and the line angle must indicate a straight path ($\theta_{line} \leq 18^\circ$). To prevent false positives from transient camera glare or sensor vibration causing state chatter, we implement a \textbf{Debounce Filter} requiring $N=5$ consecutive frames (approx. 0.15s) of validation. The core operational state $S_{horizon}$ evaluates as:
\begin{equation}
S_{horizon} = 
\begin{cases} 
\text{STRAIGHT}, & \text{if } \Sigma_{px} > 50 \text{ \& } \theta_{line} \leq 18^\circ \\
\text{CURVE}, & \text{if } \theta_{line} > 18^\circ \\
\text{UNKNOWN}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Crucially, if lighting anomalies obliterate the distal features (resulting in $\Sigma_{px} \le 50$), the system defaults to the \texttt{UNKNOWN} state rather than erroneously assuming a straight path, mitigating a potential fail-deadly acceleration into an unseen corner.

\subsubsection{Curvature-Aware Speed Controller with Asymmetric LPF}
The final longitudinal control bridges the steering geometry with the Horizon Scanner's strategic foresight. The baseline target velocity $v_{curve}$ is dictated by centripetal physics, aiming to keep the vehicle within its lateral acceleration limit $a_{lat\_max}$ (which is determined experimentally based on tire-surface friction):
\begin{equation}
    v_{curve} = \sqrt{\frac{a_{lat\_max}}{|\kappa_{eff}| + \epsilon}}
\end{equation}

The Horizon Automata dynamically overrides this baseline. If $S_{horizon} = \text{STRAIGHT}$ and the vehicle's current $\kappa_{eff} < 0.3$ (indicating structural stability on a straightaway), the target speed is increased to $v_{max}$. To guarantee absolute safety during prolonged straightaways, a \textbf{Maximum Boost Timeout} (e.g., 5.0 seconds) is strictly enforced. If the timer expires, or if $S_{horizon}$ transitions to \text{CURVE} or \texttt{UNKNOWN}, the system instantly terminates the boost and safely reverts to $v_{curve}$.

To prevent traction loss during these sudden state transitions, the raw target velocity $v_{target}$ is passed through an Asymmetric Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Low-Pass Filter:
\begin{equation}
    v(t) = (1 - \alpha) \cdot v(t-1) + \alpha \cdot v_{target}
\end{equation}
We apply $\alpha = 0.05$ for acceleration and $\alpha = 0.30$ for deceleration. Under a loss of track condition (Emergency Stop), the filter is bypassed entirely ($\alpha = 1.0$) to facilitate rapid cessation of movement.

\section{Approach 3: Smart City Perception and Event-Driven Intersection Automata}

The ``Smart City'' navigation challenge demands a highly reliable cognitive workflow to handle complex structural road scenes containing traffic signs and signal lights. To preserve computational bandwidth on the edge platform, we formalize this task into a discrete Finite State Machine (FSM) governed by a multi-variable matrix: spatial distance and visual semantic classification.

\subsection{State Space Taxonomy and Core Topology}
The behavioral control of the vehicle at any intersection is dynamically mapped by monitoring two environmental vectors: Object Distance ($D \in \{\text{Far}, \text{Near}\}$) and Sign Attributes ($S \in \{\text{Position Known}, \text{Type Known}\}$). This combinatoric matrix yields four discrete operational states:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{State 1 ($S_1$: Far + Type Known):} The vehicle is physically distant from the intersection but successfully extracts the classification type or semantic meaning of the signpost.
    \item \textbf{State 2 ($S_2$: Near + Position Known):} The vehicle has advanced close to the intersection, but due to severe perspective distortion, glare, or cropping, the camera can only track the physical coordinates/side of the signpost without verifying its category.
    \item \textbf{State 3 ($S_3$: Far + Structural Baseline):} The exploratory starting state. The vehicle defaults to continuous PID lane keeping while continuously scanning the look-ahead horizon for upcoming traffic signs or light poles.
    \item \textbf{State 4 ($S_4$: Near + Type Known):} The ultimate target state ($\geq 90\%$ spatial probability threshold). The vehicle is positioned precisely at the intersection node boundary, facing the signpost for final validation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Automata Transition Mechanics and Memory Registers}
The operational workflow is systematically engineered to enforce convergence toward State 4. The structural lifecycle is formalized as follows:
\begin{equation}
S_3 \longrightarrow \{S_1 \text{ or } S_2\} \longrightarrow S_4
\end{equation}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:smart_city_fsm}, the vehicle starts from the exploratory baseline state $S_3$, then transitions through either far-field semantic recognition ($S_1$) or near-field positional tracking ($S_2$), before reaching the final validation state $S_4$ at the intersection boundary.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{721144236_4538574843132885_8422374236532373294_n.png}
    \caption{State space transition diagram for Smart City intersection automata.}
    \label{fig:smart_city_fsm}
\end{figure}

Each individual state triggers a unique scheduling loop:

In $S_3$, the vehicle performs normal lane-tracking operations. Once a peripheral object is identified, the system transitions to either $S_1$ or $S_2$. 

When transitioning into $S_1$, the system processes the far-field visual data and instantiates a dynamic temporary guidance register, denoted as $V_{temp}$. The vehicle maintains its lane tracking sequence until the camera and localized topological node-detection algorithms signal arrival at the intersection entry boundary. 

If sensory occlusion or illumination noise disrupts classification during the physical approach, the system falls back to $S_2$. In this fail-safe phase, the vehicle bypasses real-time classification and forces navigation commands by extracting the historical direction stored inside $V_{temp}$.

Conversely, when the system moves from $S_1$ directly to $S_4$, the vehicle executes a pre-programmed spatial realignment, physically pivoting the camera directly toward the bounding coordinates of the signpost. The system executes a near-field classification, cross-referencing the result against $V_{temp}$. If the outputs match, the vehicle fulfills the instruction. If a structural conflict arises, the real-time classification at $S_4$ takes supreme priority.

\subsection{Hybrid Semantic Perception: YOLOv8 Segmentation and Hough Circle Transform}
To achieve robust traffic sign recognition under varying illumination and perspective distortions in the Smart City environment, we implement a hybrid perception pipeline:

\textit{Geometric Extraction via Hough Circle Transform:} Before invoking heavy neural networks, the system leverages the Hough Gradient Method (\texttt{cv2.HoughCircles}) on the pre-processed grayscale image to rapidly detect circular contours. This acts as a highly efficient Region of Interest (ROI) proposal mechanism, ensuring that only geometrically valid candidate regions containing traffic signs are processed further.

\textit{Semantic Classification via YOLOv8 Segmentation:} Once a circular ROI is validated, a quantized YOLOv8 segmentation model evaluates the localized patch. By operating strictly on the Hough-proposed cropped regions rather than the full camera frame, the YOLOv8 model drastically reduces VRAM consumption and inference latency. This synergistic pipeline ensures high-confidence classification of directional and regulatory signs (e.g., \textit{``Turn Right''}, \textit{``No Entry''}) while preserving the strict real-time constraints of the edge device.

\subsection{Preemptive Traffic Light Handling Hierarchy}
Across all operational states, traffic light state registration runs with absolute execution priority. Upon converging at the intersection boundary ($S_4$), the system enforces a strict hierarchical policy before evaluating any signpost attributes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Red Light Assertion:} The FSM instantly overrides steering commands and triggers immediate braking. The vehicle remains locked in a stationary read loop. Sign verification is suppressed until a Green light condition is met.
    \item \textbf{Green Light Clearance:} The vehicle instantly transitions to sign-alignment, cross-references the semantic classification with $V_{temp}$, and executes the directional trajectory.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Plan}
To validate the proposed architecture, we establish a rigorous testing framework on the physical Jetson platform.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics \& Baseline}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frames Per Second (FPS):} Must sustain $\geq 25$ FPS to satisfy real-time processing limits under edge-computing constraints.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Track Error (CTE) \& Curvature ($\kappa$):} Evaluating the stability of the steering controller during high-speed cornering.
    \item \textbf{Lap Time Optimization:} Measuring the time reduction achieved by the Horizon Scanner's predictive acceleration compared to static cruise speed.
    \item \textbf{Task Success Rate:} Percentage of successful traffic sign recognitions and intersection handling.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Baseline Method:} A purely reactive pipeline utilizing standard static OpenCV HSV masking with fixed-speed PID steering (Version 2.0).

\subsection{Data Collection \& Log Analysis}
A custom 3-panel visualizer (Raw Image, Color Mask, BEV Output) is developed to run in parallel with the vehicle control loop. Telemetry data (Curvature, $S_{horizon}$ state, EMA Throttle, and Steering Angle) will be logged to local CSV files. Post-run analysis will graph velocity transitions relative to track geometry to tune the EMA $\alpha$ parameters and shift thresholds.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
To rigorously quantify the contribution of each module, an ablation study will be conducted comparing four architectural variants:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{V2.0 Baseline:} Static HSV + Reactive PID + Fixed Speed.
    \item \textbf{V3.0:} V2.0 + BEV Transform + Curvature-aware Speed ($v_{curve}$).
    \item \textbf{V3.1-A:} V3.0 + Dual-Zone LAB Constraint + Look-Ahead Horizon Scanner.
    \item \textbf{V3.1-B (Full):} V3.1-A + Asymmetric EMA Speed Filter.
\end{itemize}
We expect the full V3.1-B architecture to yield the lowest Lap Time while maintaining or reducing the overall CTE compared to the baseline.

\section{Implementation Plan}
The project will be executed over a 4-week agile sprint:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Week 1:} Camera calibration, Perspective Transform (BEV), and Dual-Zone HSV+LAB segmentation tuning.
    \item \textbf{Week 2:} Metric curvature extraction and Smart City sign template matching.
    \item \textbf{Week 3:} Look-Ahead Horizon Scanner automata, Asymmetric EMA Speed Controller, and Traffic Light priority logic.
    \item \textbf{Week 4:} Full system integration, 3-panel dashboard deployment, and physical sa-ban stress testing.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Technical Risks \& Mitigation:}
1) \textit{Illumination Anomalies:} Unpredictable glare causing false curve detections. \textit{Mitigation:} Implementation of the CIE-LAB $a^{*}$ constraint and the \texttt{UNKNOWN} fail-safe default state. 
2) \textit{Thermal Throttling:} Overheating CPU/GPU causing FPS drops. \textit{Mitigation:} Enforce deterministic CV algorithms over heavy Neural Networks to keep utilization below 60\%, combined with active cooling.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\section{Expected Outcome \& Limitation}
\textbf{Expected Outcome:} A structured, low-latency navigation system capable of proactive acceleration on straightaways and controlled deceleration into curves. By fusing dual-zone color filtering and BEV geometry, the architecture is expected to outperform traditional reactive PID systems in both lap time and overall stability.

\textbf{Limitations:} The Horizon Scanner relies on static pixel regions ($Y \in [215, 260]$). If the camera's physical pitch angle shifts due to vibration or impacts, the distal ROI mapping becomes misaligned, which may require mechanical recalibration. Furthermore, extreme motion blur at high speeds (e.g., $0.80$ m/s) may degrade the $a^{*}$ channel extraction, requiring appropriate shutter speed adjustments.

% ---------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
This proposal details a hybrid perception architecture decoupling strategic FSMs from tactical PID control. By mathematically formalizing the look-ahead heuristics and utilizing Dual-Zone vision, the system is designed to improve control stability and hardware efficiency, providing a robust foundation for the competition.
